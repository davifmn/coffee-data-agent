# Brazilian specialty coffee regions

Brazil is the world's largest coffee producer. Most specialty Arabica comes from the
states of Minas Gerais (MG), São Paulo (SP), Bahia (BA), Espírito Santo (ES) and
Paraná (PR). Several regions have official geographical indications (IG), which tie
the coffee's name to its place of origin and production rules.

## Cerrado Mineiro (MG)

A plateau in western Minas Gerais at roughly 800 to 1,300 m. Clearly separated rainy
and dry seasons give a uniform harvest and reliable drying, and the flat terrain
allows mechanised harvesting. It was the first Brazilian coffee region granted a
Denomination of Origin. Typical cups: chocolate, caramel, nuts and medium acidity.

## Sul de Minas (MG)

The largest producing region in Brazil, with rolling hills at about 800 to 1,400 m.
It produces everything from commodity coffee to award-winning micro-lots; naturals and
pulped naturals with sweet, chocolate and fruit notes dominate.

## Mantiqueira de Minas (MG)

A mountainous area in the Serra da Mantiqueira, with farms up to and above 1,500 m.
Cool temperatures slow cherry ripening, which favours complex, sweet and bright cups.
The region is a frequent winner of national quality competitions and has a
geographical indication.

## Matas de Minas (MG)

Steep, humid hills in eastern Minas Gerais farmed mostly by small family producers.
Hand picking is the norm. Cups are often sweet with citrus and floral notes.

## Alta Mogiana (SP)

A high plateau region in north-eastern São Paulo state with a long coffee history and
a geographical indication. Known for sweet, full-bodied coffees with low to medium
acidity.

## Chapada Diamantina (BA)

High-altitude farms in Bahia, at about 1,000 to 1,500 m, that became known for top
competition scores. Cups can be very sweet and fruity.

## Caparaó (MG/ES)

Mountains around the Pico da Bandeira on the border of Minas Gerais and Espírito
Santo. High altitude and small farms produce bright, complex coffees.

## Montanhas do Espírito Santo (ES)

Espírito Santo is best known for Conilon (Robusta), but its mountain areas grow
high-quality Arabica, often washed or pulped natural.

## Norte Pioneiro do Paraná (PR)

Lower-altitude region (around 600 to 1,000 m) in northern Paraná with a geographical
indication. Coffees are usually sweet and mild.
