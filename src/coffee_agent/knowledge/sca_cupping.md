# SCA cupping and specialty coffee scores

## What makes a coffee "specialty"

Under the Specialty Coffee Association (SCA) cupping protocol, a coffee scoring 80
points or more out of 100 is considered specialty grade, provided the green sample
also passes the defect limits. Commonly used bands are:

- 80.00 to 84.99: very good
- 85.00 to 89.99: excellent
- 90.00 and above: outstanding

Coffees below 80 points are not marketed as specialty.

## The traditional score sheet

Cuppers score six quality attributes on a scale from 6 to 10 in quarter-point steps:
fragrance/aroma, flavour, aftertaste, acidity, body and balance. Uniformity, clean cup
and sweetness are scored per cup (2 points for each of five cups). An overall
impression score is added, and defects found in the cups are subtracted.

## Cupping protocol

Samples are roasted light to medium within 24 hours before cupping and rested for at
least 8 hours. The standard ratio is 8.25 g of coffee per 150 ml of water at about
93 °C. After about 4 minutes the crust is broken, the surface is cleaned and cuppers
taste the coffee repeatedly as it cools, because some defects only show at lower
temperatures.

## Q Graders

Licensed Q Arabica Graders are cuppers certified by the Coffee Quality Institute.
Scores given by Q Graders are the usual reference when buyers and sellers agree on the
grade of a lot.

## Newer assessment

In 2024 the SCA introduced the Coffee Value Assessment (CVA), which separates
descriptive notes from affective (preference) scores. Many producers and marketplaces
still quote scores on the traditional 100-point scale.
