# Coffee processing methods

Processing is everything that happens between picking the ripe coffee cherry and
storing the dried green bean. It changes the flavour of the cup as much as variety
or altitude does.

## Natural (dry) process

The whole cherry is dried with the fruit still around the seed, on patios or raised
beds, for two to four weeks. Sugars and fruit compounds migrate into the bean, so
naturals tend to have more body, sweetness and fruity notes (berries, tropical fruit,
chocolate). The risk is uneven drying or over-fermentation, which gives boozy or
fermented off-flavours. Natural is the most common process in Brazil, where dry
harvest seasons make patio drying practical.

## Washed (wet) process

The skin and pulp are removed mechanically, the remaining mucilage is broken down by
fermentation in water tanks (typically 12 to 48 hours) and washed off before drying.
Washed coffees usually taste cleaner and brighter, with more pronounced acidity and a
lighter body. The method needs a lot of water and infrastructure.

## Pulped natural (cereja descascado)

A Brazilian technique, called "cereja descascado" in Portuguese: the skin is removed
but most of the sticky mucilage is left on the bean while it dries. The result sits
between washed and natural: sweeter and fuller than a washed coffee, cleaner than a
natural. It became widespread in Brazil in the 1990s.

## Honey process

Close to pulped natural, but producers control how much mucilage stays on the bean.
Names describe the colour the bean takes while drying: white and yellow honey keep
less mucilage, red and black honey keep more and dry more slowly, producing sweeter,
heavier and more fruit-forward cups.

## Anaerobic and experimental fermentation

Cherries or pulped beans ferment in sealed tanks without oxygen, often with controlled
temperature and time, before drying. These lots can show intense, unusual flavours
(cinnamon, wine, tropical fruit) and often score high in competitions, but results
vary a lot between batches and some buyers find them polarising.

## Drying and resting

Whatever the process, beans are dried to about 10 to 12 percent moisture and then
rested ("repouso") for 30 to 60 days in the parchment before milling. Resting
stabilises moisture and improves cup consistency.
