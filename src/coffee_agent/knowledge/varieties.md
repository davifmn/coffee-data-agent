# Arabica varieties grown in Brazil

## Bourbon (Amarelo and Vermelho)

A classic Arabica lineage known for sweetness and balanced acidity. Yellow Bourbon
(Bourbon Amarelo) is especially associated with Brazilian specialty lots. It yields
less than modern cultivars, so it is usually sold at a premium.

## Mundo Novo

A natural cross between Bourbon and Typica-derived Sumatra selected in Brazil.
Vigorous and productive, with good cup quality; it is a parent of Catuaí and
Topázio.

## Catuaí (Amarelo and Vermelho)

A compact, high-yield cross of Mundo Novo and Caturra developed by the Instituto
Agronômico de Campinas (IAC). It is one of the most planted varieties in Brazil. Cups
are usually clean, sweet and chocolatey.

## Topázio

A cross of Mundo Novo and Catuaí Amarelo with yellow fruit, valued for productivity
and a sweet cup.

## Arara

A Brazilian yellow-fruited cultivar with good rust resistance that has become popular
among specialty producers for its sweetness and fruit notes.

## Catucaí and Yellow Icatu

Cultivars that carry Timor Hybrid ancestry for disease resistance. Good examples can
reach specialty scores, especially in high-altitude regions.

## Acaiá

A Mundo Novo selection with large beans, often chosen by specialty buyers who look for
bigger screen sizes.

## Geisha (Gesha)

An Ethiopian landrace made famous in Panama. Known for floral, jasmine and bergamot
notes and very high cupping scores. Rare in Brazil and usually the most expensive lot
in any catalogue.
