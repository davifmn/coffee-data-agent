# Roast levels

## Light roast

Roasting ends soon after first crack (the popping sound heard at roughly 196 °C bean
temperature). Light roasts keep more of the origin character: acidity, floral and
fruit notes. Most competition and high-scoring specialty coffees are presented light.

## Medium roast

Roasting continues for a while after first crack but stops before second crack.
Medium roasts balance acidity and sweetness and bring out caramel and chocolate notes.
This is the most popular level for Brazilian specialty coffee.

## Medium-dark and dark roast

Roasting approaches or reaches second crack (around 224 °C). Bitterness and
roast-derived flavours (dark chocolate, smoke) increase while acidity and origin
character decrease. Dark roasts are common in traditional espresso blends.

## Resting after roasting

Freshly roasted beans release carbon dioxide. Filter coffee usually tastes best from
about 3 to 21 days after roasting, espresso after about 7 days. Store whole beans in
an airtight, opaque bag with a one-way valve, away from heat and light.
